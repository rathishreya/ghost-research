# Visual Concepts — The End of De Minimis: The 2026 Landed-Cost Reset

> **v2 rebuild (2026-05-23):** The rendered deck (`prompts.md` + `assets/`) was rebuilt on the approved Template Library (`.claude/skills/_shared/template-library.md`) per Shreyanshi's taste review — thin red frame, masthead, bottom-anchored column, blob structures, corrected footer sizes, CTA on every card. **The two former video concepts (C02, C09) are now stills** (C02 → Pull-Quote, C09 → Journal-Cover launch); videos are paused. C08 became a clean stat-hero (was a data table). Concept *intent* below is unchanged; only the execution template changed.

## Strategy summary
The campaign's one insight — *everyone knows the rule changes, nobody has the math* — drives a visual system built on the artifacts of the actual decision: the cross-border **parcel** under customs tape, the **landed-cost P&L** with one line about to break, the **shipping-lane map** that re-routes, and the **per-band cost table**. No stock "executive at a laptop." No AI-generated humans (per the v2 brand guideline). What makes these Ghost and not generic B2B: the deep-indigo editorial grade, the single red focal point per frame (a customs line, a €3 cell, a margin delta), and the discipline that Week-1 frames withhold every number while Week-2 frames reveal them.

**Theme mix required:** 12 concepts — **10 dark + 2 LIGHT-theme** (C04 and C11). Light theme uses `#F8F8FF` paper, `#06062D` ink, red `#EF4444` unchanged, icon mark unchanged. Light lanes here: the "€150 → ?" stat reveal (C04) and the "Which SKUs first?" prioritization card (C11) — both read as premium editorial against the dark-mode majority.

**No-people rule:** Every hero is an object / document / data UI / typographic frame. This satisfies both the "no AI-generated faces" lock and the swap test, because the props (customs tape, €3 parcel line, China→EU lane, price-band table) are recognizable *only* to a cross-border-commerce buyer.

## Concept 01 — "The Parcel at the Border"

**Maps to calendar:** Day 1, Week 1 (Mon 1 Jun)
**Style preset:** Suspense + Instagram
**Platform:** Instagram (Meta) + X
**Format:** Static 1:1 (1080×1080)
**Hook used:** Hook 1 (time-decay)
**Ad copy (from brief):**
- Headline: "On 1 July, your landed-cost model expires."
- Body: "On 1 July the EU ends the €150 de-minimis exemption and adds €3 per parcel. Get the landed-cost model the day it drops."
- CTA: "Notify me on launch →"

### Visual specification
**Subject:** A single brown cross-border shipping parcel, wrapped with a customs declaration form and a strip of red-and-white customs tape, sitting alone on a dark surface. A faint embossed "1 JULY" stamp catches the light on the box's side. The parcel is the buyer's daily reality — the unit the entire de-minimis rule is measured in.
**Setting:** A near-black sorting-table surface, deep indigo shadow, one shaft of cool light from upper-left. No people, no warehouse clutter — just the parcel, isolated like evidence.

**⚠️ Swap test:** Replace the report with any other Ghost title — does a customs-taped parcel stamped "1 JULY" still fit? No. PASS (parcel + customs tape + date are de-minimis-specific).

**Composition:**
- Subject placement: parcel in lower-right third, emerging from shadow
- Negative space: upper-left two-thirds (deep indigo) for headline
- Depth: parcel sharp, background falls to black

**Lighting:** Single soft cool key from upper-left; long shadow to the right; red customs tape is the only warm/saturated element.
**Color grade:** Deep indigo / navy. Shadows lifted toward `#06062D`. Red tape pulled to `#EF4444`.

**On-screen elements (added in design tool, NOT generated):**
- Headline: "On 1 July, your landed-cost model expires." — Oranienbaum, white, upper-left
- Sub-text: "DE-MINIMIS ENDS · MODEL DROPS 7 JUNE" — Manrope, white 70%, kicker above headline
- **Ghost Research icon mark — MANDATORY.** `assets/brand/logo-mark.svg`, top-left, 1:1 placement x≈70 y≈70, 88–104px.
- CTA button: "Notify me on launch →" — `#EF4444` fill, white Manrope semibold, lower-left

**Why this works:** The lone parcel as "evidence" matches Hook 1's quiet dread — the everyday object is about to cost more and the reader can't yet see how much. Suspense, not alarm.
