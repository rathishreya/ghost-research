# Visual Concepts (Preset Edition) — The AI Energy Demand Atlas 2026

## Strategy summary

This deck re-skins the AI Energy Demand Atlas campaign through the new **Style Preset
Library** (`.claude/skills/_shared/style-presets.md`). Week 1 (hype, report not public)
runs **CINEMATIC + SUSPENSE** — withheld-information tension is the perfect register for
the named-villain ($147B / 9 utilities) and insider-POV (PJM nodes) hooks, and the
suspense "redaction" motif is *literally honest* because the report doesn't exist yet.
Week 2 (launch, report live) runs **UI-HEAVY + SaaS** — the proof is the data product
itself: trade-level exposure maps, repricing tiles, FERC-traceable source rows rendered
as real product UI, which is exactly what separates Ghost from IEA macro decks.

What makes these Ghost-branded, not generic energy ads: the deep-indigo→near-black grade,
Oranienbaum/Manrope only, one red `#EF4444` accent per frame, the icon mark on every
frame, info-gap headlines, and topic-specific props (PJM topology, substation
transformers, FERC filing rows, utility tickers) that pass the swap test.

**🔒 Two hard-rule applications baked in (these override the older brief copy):**
1. **No price in any ad copy.** The brief (created 2026-05-18) still shows "$500" in CTAs. Per the 2026-05-19 hard rule, **every CTA here is price-free** — "Read the Atlas →", not "Read the Atlas — $500 →". Price reveals on the landing page only. The "Bloomberg $30K vs Atlas $500" anchor moves to the landing page, not the creative.
2. **Icon mark on every frame**, top-left, red rounded-square + white inset circle — not the text wordmark.

Split: **7 hype-safe (Week 1)** + **6 content-active (Week 2)**. 11 dark + 2 light (C05, C08).

## Concept 01 — "Nine Names, Redacted"

**Phase:** 🔒 HYPE-SAFE (Week 1)
**Maps to calendar:** Day 1 (LinkedIn) + Day 2 (X variant)
**Style preset:** Suspense + Meta/Instagram-safe (built 1:1 for LI feed, recropped for X)
**Platform:** LinkedIn + X
**Format:** Static 1:1 (1080×1080)
**Hook used:** Hook 1 — Named villain ($147B → 9 utilities)
**Ad copy (from brief, price-stripped):**
- Headline: "$147B in announced hyperscaler capex just landed on 9 utility balance sheets."
- Body: "9 utilities absorbing $147B. We mapped the exposure. Caspr-powered, expert-vetted, full citation trail. Atlas drops June 1."
- CTA: "Get on the drop list →"

### Phase compliance check
- HYPE-SAFE: no findings — the 9 utilities are *named-but-hidden* (redacted). The $147B is a public capex figure, not a report finding. ✅
- Surfaces no report element. ✅

### Visual specification
**Subject:** A printed ledger / balance-sheet page on a dark desk, nine line-items running down the page — eight of them blacked out with clean redaction bars, the ninth half-emerging from shadow. No people (suspense preset: object as hero).
**Setting:** Near-black desk surface, a single sheet of heavy paper under a hard raking light; faint reflection of transmission-line silhouettes in the desk's polished edge. Topic-specific prop: the redacted page is a *utility balance-sheet*, not a generic document.
**Composition:** Document occupies lower-right two-thirds, angled; the redaction bars draw the eye down the page to the one un-redacted (but still illegible) row. Negative space upper-left for headline.
**Lighting:** Single hard light from camera-right at a low angle — film-noir raking light that makes the redaction bars cast micro-shadows. Deep falloff into black.
**Color grade:** Deep indigo / near-black. Whites pulled to `#F8F8FF`. One faint red edge-glow on the un-redacted row only.
**On-screen elements (added in post, NOT generated):**
- Headline overlay: "$147B just landed on 9 utility balance sheets." — Oranienbaum, white, upper-left
- Sub-text: "The grid isn't ready. The atlas drops June 1." — Manrope regular, white 80%, below headline
- Icon mark: top-left x≈70 y≈70, 96px
- CTA button: "Get on the drop list →" — `#EF4444` fill, white Manrope semibold, lower-left

**Why this works:** Suspense preset turns the named-villain hook into a visual gap — eight redacted rows scream "which nine?" and the reader cannot close the gap without the atlas. Quiet authority via the noir grade, no AI clichés.
